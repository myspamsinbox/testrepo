Meet the thorny devil (Moloch horridus), a spiky little lizard of Australia's deserts, only about 20 cm long.

Rain is rare where it lives, so it doesn't need a puddle. Tiny grooves run between its scales, and when it touches dew or damp sand, the water creeps along them by capillary action, all the way to the corners of its mouth.

Under threat it tucks its real head between its front legs and shows a spiky knob on its neck instead: a fake head. It can even shift colour, darker in the cool morning and paler as the day heats up.

The twist: its menu is almost nothing but ants. Thousands a day, picked up one at a time. 🐜
